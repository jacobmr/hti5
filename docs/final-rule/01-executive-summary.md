## I. Executive Summary

### A. Purpose of Deregulatory Action

Since the inception of the ONC Health IT Certification Program (Certification Program) (42 U.S.C. 300jj–11(c)(5)), ONC has aimed to implement and administer the Certification Program in the least burdensome manner that supports an administration’s policy goals. Throughout the years, we have worked to improve the Certification Program with a focus on ways to reduce burden, offer flexibility to both developers and providers, and support innovation.

Over the past 15 years, we have established a set of regulatory requirements that have provided a foundation for our voluntary Certification Program. As we look to the future, we reviewed the existing requirements and made updates. Through both formal notice and comment rulemaking and informal engagements, we received suggestions on how to update the Certification Program to meet current market needs. This final rule includes deregulatory actions that: take into consideration suggestions and comments received on the Health Data, Technology, and Interoperability: ASTP/ONC Deregulatory Actions To Unleash Prosperity Proposed Rule (90 FR 60970) (“Proposed Rule”); account for the current and future state of technology; reduce burden on health IT developers; and promote innovation for health IT developers, providers, and other interested parties.

These efforts directly align with Administration goals as outlined in EO 14192 (Unleashing Prosperity through Deregulation)1 and EO 14267 (Reducing Anti-Competitive Regulatory Barriers).2 By removing burdensome and unnecessary requirements of the Certification Program, this final rule supports providers in caring for their patients. Developers of certified health IT will have more time to support providers’ specific technological needs. Further, by increasing competition in the certified health IT market that will come from reduced barriers to entry for the certification of health IT, providers also will have more choices to meet their health IT needs.

America’s interoperability arc in healthcare has bent decisively toward Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources (FHIR®)-based application programming interfaces (APIs). An open, interoperable health data ecosystem invites entrepreneurship, drives innovation, and cements American leadership and competitiveness in the expanding digital health marketplace. As discussed in this deregulatory final rule, we aggressively reduce and remove long-standing functionality-oriented and non-FHIR-based certification criteria from the

### B. Summary of Major Provisions

For the purposes of this final rule, we have reviewed and responded to comments on a narrowed set of proposals from the Proposed Rule. Comments received in response to other Proposed Rule proposals not discussed in this final rule remain under review and consideration. The Proposed Rule proposals not discussed in this final rule may, in the future, be included in subsequent final rules or as revised proposals in subsequent proposed rules.

#### 1. Health Information Technology Standards, Implementation Specifications, and Certification

Criteria and Certification Programs for Health Information Technology (Part 170)

##### a. Certification Criteria for Health Information Technology

In the Proposed Rule, we identified certification criteria for proposed removal or revision (90 FR 60973). We noted that the removal or revision of the identified certification criteria would reduce burden and costs for health IT developers and clinicians by eliminating the need to design and meet specific certification functionalities; to prepare, test, and certify health IT in certain instances; and to maintain conformance to certification requirements. As explained in the Proposed Rule, we have observed that over time certification is likely no longer a primary factor driving improvements or compliance in particular areas. Throughout this final rule, we include finalized revisions and removals of certification criteria that we believe are no longer necessary to advance interoperability and no longer provide a strong market driver for initial adoption and implementation of the certified functionalities.

In the Proposed Rule, out of the 60 certification criteria that are currently in effect, we proposed to remove 34 and revise seven. In this final rule, we have finalized the removal of 14

certification criteria and the revision of seven criteria. Of the revised criteria, one of the more substantial revisions reduces the scope of the “decision support interventions” certification criterion to fully remove the super intelligence “model card” requirements in § 170.315(b)(11)(iv) for source attribute support and § 170.315(b)(11)(v) for source attribute access and modification. We have retained and made no changes to 39 certification criteria, which include new and revised criteria that were adopted in the Health Data, Technology, and Interoperability: Electronic Prior Authorization and Real-Time Prescription Benefit (HTI-4) final rule (90 FR 37130). The HTI-4 final rule was a “rider” in the FY 2026 CMS Hospital Inpatient Prospective Payment System and Long-Term Care Hospital Prospective Payment System final rule (90 FR 36536). The new and revised criteria that were finalized in the HTI-4 final rule were: three new electronic prior authorization API criteria, a new “real-time prescription benefit” criterion,6 a revised e-prescribing criterion, and two criteria supporting modular API capabilities.

##### b. Standards and Implementation Specifications for Health Information Technology

As proposed in the Proposed Rule (90 FR 60973), in some instances where we finalize the removal or revision of a certain certification criterion, we also finalize the removal of the standard(s) that is referenced by the certification criterion. In some cases, we have finalized the removal of standards consistent with a certification criterion transition period until January 1, 2027. In one circumstance, we retain a standard that was referenced in a certification criterion that we removed for purposes of health IT interoperability alignment.7 We also finalized the removal of standards that are outdated and no longer referenced in the Certification Program.

### C. Severability

It is our intent that each provision of this final rule be severable from other provisions of this final rule. This means that if any provision of this final rule is held to be invalid or unenforceable facially, or as applied to any person or plaintiff, or stayed pending further judicial or agency action, we intend that such provision shall be severable from other provisions of this final rule, and from rules and regulations currently in effect. We provided notice of this intent in the HTI-5 Proposed Rule (90 FR 60974). It is also our intent that, unless such provision shall be held to be utterly invalid or unenforceable, it be construed to give the provision maximum effect permitted by law including in the application of the provision to other persons not similarly situated or to other, dissimilar circumstances from those where the provision may be held to be invalid or unenforceable.

In this final rule, we have finalized provisions that are intended to and will operate independently of each other, even if multiple of them serve the same or similar general purpose(s) or policy goal(s). Where a provision is necessarily dependent on another, the context generally makes that clear (such as by cross-reference to a particular standard, requirement, condition, or pre-requisite). Where a provision that is dependent on one that is stayed or held invalid or unenforceable (as described in the preceding paragraph) is included in a subparagraph, paragraph, or section within part 170 of 45 CFR, our intention is that other provisions of such subparagraph(s), paragraph(s), or section(s) operate independently of said provision and would remain in effect.

If any revision to any provision of part 170 (such as a revision to a certification criterion in part 170) were stayed or held to be invalid or unenforceable as applied to any person, plaintiff, or circumstance, we explained our intent that such provision—and any section or paragraph of part 170 that may reference such provision—be construed to give the provision maximum effect permitted by law including in the application of the provision to other persons not similarly

situated or to other, dissimilar circumstances from those where the provision may be held to be invalid or unenforceable.

### D. Costs and Benefits

The Regulatory Impact Analysis (RIA) includes an updated analysis of the costs and benefits associated with the finalized proposals in this final rule. The finalized deregulatory actions provide substantial relief for developers of certified health IT and significant cost savings realized from the revision and removal of certain Certification Program requirements. The cost savings have been estimated using the best available data and modeled on the costs estimated and finalized in prior ONC rulemakings. The finalized deregulatory actions reduce the effort for developers of certified health IT to meet ongoing Certification Program requirements and reduce entry barriers for new Certification Program participants. The finalized actions, importantly, return resources back to developers of certified health IT to innovate with their technology, improve interoperability and third-party integration, and focus on the needs of their customers. We do not expect costs to be associated with these finalized deregulatory actions. We expect the finalized deregulatory actions to result in significant benefits for health IT developers, providers, ONC-Authorized Certification Bodies (ONC-ACBs), ONC-Authorized Testing Laboratories (ONC-ATLs), and ONC. Generally, these benefits are in the form of cost and time savings and reductions in administrative burden to comply with Certification Program requirements. The finalized actions reduce the scope and breadth of Certification Program requirements. The analysis included in this final rule indicates that the finalized updates to the Certification Program would result in $0 in implementation and compliance costs on developers of certified health IT. However, we estimated some costs for reviewers who reviewed this final rule. We estimated those costs to be $1,293,508 in total for an estimated 616 reviewers, which include developers, medical associations, and other health and health IT community members. We did not quantify benefits for this final rule and instead rely on our calculation of cost savings to developers of certified health IT to quantify in dollars the time and effort developers would

have expended had these requirements remained in effect in perpetuity. In total, consistent with analytic guidance on EO 14192, this finalized rulemaking would result in a present value of cost savings of $1.55 billion and annualized net cost savings of $108.1 million in 2024 dollars and discounted at a rate of 7%, beginning in 2027 and in perpetuity.

# HHS-ONC-2025-0005-0292 - attachment text

Feb 27, 2026 
Comment to HTI-5 NPRM published on Dec 29, 2025 
In general, I concur with the elimination of the redundant governmental validations of an 
HIT module’s capabilities as proposed in this NPRM for the ASTP/ONC Certiﬁcation 
program.  Streamlining reporting to multiple governmental agencies regarding the same 
product functionality is always a move in the right direction of reducing administrative 
burden on both the healthcare delivery entities and the HIT developer community.  Thank 
you ASTP/ONC for looking outside your program at a detailed level to the programs from 
your partner agencies, e.g. CMS, OMB, etc to succinctly identify program overlaps and 
adjust accordingly. 
However, the following earmarked criteria removals and revisions elicit feedback as they 
may be reducing the burden for the HIT Developers but have the potential of weakening the 
overall certiﬁcation program deliverables for the ultimate customers the program is 
intended to help, the care provider and the patient.    
1. Real World Testing  [45 CFR 170.405] 
The proposed rollback of this validation process for all non-FHIR based criteria 
would eliminate the only mechanism in the program of documented adherence to 
certiﬁcation criteria in practice.  HIT modules are typically in a continual update 
cycle.  RWT on an ongoing basis ensures a continual 3rd party review of the products 
certiﬁed requirements on behalf of the Care Providers and Patients.  Adherence, or 
the lack thereof, to this Condition of Certiﬁcation reporting requirement, also serves 
as trigger for ONC-ACB surveillance considerations.   
The proposed revision to the RWT requirement of eliminating the RWT Plan 
submission, is a positive action to reduce administrative burden for both the HIT 
Developers and the ONC-ACBs.  I suggest that this be the only action in the revision 
for RWT.     
2. Transitions of Care § 170.315(b((1) and Consolidated CDA Creation Performance § 
170.315(g)(6) 
In § 170.315(b((1) you propose to remove the requirements in § 170.315(b)(1)(i) “send 
and receive via edge protocol ” and in § 170.315(b)(1)(iii) “create” and to simplify the 
requirements in §  170.315(b)(1)(ii) “validate and display ” to focus on the ability to 
receive and validate C-CDA documents. We propose to remove the requirements in 
§ 170.315(b)(1)(ii)(B) and (C). We also propose to move the requirements in 
§ 170.315(b)(1)(ii)(A) “validate C -CDA conformance — system performance ” to 

§ 170.315(b)(1)(i) and to rename § 170.315(b)(1) to read “transitions of care—receive 
and validate. ” 
What I don’t read in these proposed revisions is the criterion for the creation of any 
FHIR-based “bundle”, potentially in lieu of a C -CDA document, to be a requirement 
of an originating care facility of a transition of care, e.g. an acute care hospital.  
This “creation” of a care summary  requirement, albeit C -CDA based currently, is in 
the criterion Consolidated CDA Creation Performance § 170.315(g)(6 .  As you noted 
in your section for this, “The Consolidated CDA creation performance” certiﬁcation 
criterion helps to ensure the proper conformance of Consolidated CDAs (C-CDAs) for 
transition of care and referral summaries that are sent to and received from external 
organizations. Given the emphasis to move to FHIR -based data exchanges, I 
recommend that instead of just “removing the Consolidated CDA creation 
performance certiﬁcation criterion in §  170.315(g)(6) to … contribute to reducing the 
effort health IT developers and health care providers are focusing on C -CDA support 
and allow for redirection of efforts toward greater use of FHIR -based standards in 
health IT, that the § 170.315(g)(6) criteria be revised or a new certiﬁcation criterion be 
created to specially call out the requirement to create a FHIR-based summary of care 
to be used in transitions of care and referrals. 
3. Care Plan § 170.315(b)(9) 
In the NPRM, you  “have concluded that removing the certiﬁcation criterion in 
§ 170.315(b)(9) would reduce burden for health IT developers and health care 
providers while still supporting our goals. Additionally, as discussed previously, 
moving away from C -CDA-based requirements in the Certiﬁcation Program would 
help promote greater innovation and advancement in this area.”   
Similar to #2 above, I recommend that in lieu of removing the criteria, that you instead 
revise it or create a new certiﬁcation criterion to require that the use of a FHIR-based 
care plan, i.e one of th ose that  “ASTP/ONC recently highlighted the development of 
FHIR-based care plan IGs, including the Multiple Chronic Condition eCare Plan and 
the Electronic Long Term Services & Support Care Plan IGs, in a January 2025 
standards bulletin” 
Lastly, regarding the two choices for criteria requirement verifcation, testing by an ONC-ACL 
or by Attestation by the HIT Developer, the lat ter option is lacking the veriﬁcation strength 
provided by formal testing by an test lab.  It is understood that creating testing tools  in 
support of the ﬁrst option is a considerable, and costly, undertak ing by the ASTP/ONC.  As 
such, it is appropriate for the ASTP/ONC to have moved some of the criteria (and even more 

of them in this NPRM) to Attestation.  I recommend, however, that to increase the value and 
strength of the Attestation process, that the testing event noted in a criteria’s Attestation be 
one from a n industry, e.g.  SDO, and ASTP/ONC recognized testing organization that 
conducts testing with 3 rd party validation processes and public documentation of test 
results, such as IHE International Connectathons.  Providing a list of ASTP/ONC recognized 
testing organizations and testing events would provide an efficient validation resource for 
ONC-ACBs to use as part of their product certiﬁcation. 
 
Thank you for your consideration of these comments.

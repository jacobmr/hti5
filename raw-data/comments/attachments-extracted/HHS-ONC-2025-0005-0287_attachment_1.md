I. Information Blocking —  Manner Exception (45 C.F.R. § 171.301) 
The proposed revisions to the Manner Exception raise significant operational concerns for 
nonprofit HIEs and HDUs that rely on standardized participation agreements to enable multi-party 
exchange. 
ASTP/ONC proposes to revise § 171.301(a)(2) by adding a new subparagraph (iii), which would 
require that any contract related to EHI access, exchange, or use be at market rate, not be a 
contract of adhesion, and not contain unconscionable terms. In the alt ernative, ASTP/ONC is 
considering removing § 171.301(a)(2) and instead requiring Actors to satisfy the Fees and Licensing 
Exceptions to qualify for the Manner Exception. 
Contexture supports  and engages in  fair contracting practices . We recommend relying  on 
compliance with the Fees and Licensing Exceptions, rather than expanding § 171.301(a)(2) to 
impose new contractual conditions.  This preserves clarity, relies on established guardrails, and 
enables HIEs  and HDUs to rely on standardized participation agreements essential to 
interoperability.  
The proposed fair market value (FMV) requirement is not workable for nonprofit HIE/HDU 
models. In the absence of meaningful industry benchmarks, FMV is difficult to apply to cost-based, 
community-governed exchange models.  Compliance with the  Fees Exception appropriately 
addresses both cost-allocation and fairness concerns.  
Separately, the proposed definition of “contract of adhesion” does not reflect how trusted, multi-
party exchange operates.  The definition  focuses on form — standardized or “take-it-or-leave-it” 
contracts— rather than purpose or effect . Standardized agreements, designed for multi -party 
networks with thousands of Participants and that are free of unconscionable terms, are necessary 
to reduce friction, ensure predictable obligations, and scale interoperable networks. Certain terms, 
such as security requirements and interoperability specifications, must apply uniformly for an HIE 
or HDU to function . Requiring custom negotiations with every participant would increase 
complexity and cost for nonprofit, community -based HIEs and HDUs , and could undermine the 
very interoperability the rule seeks to promote.

Should ASTP/ONC decide to finalize new subparagraph (iii), additional safeguards are necessary 
to avoid unintended consequences: 
• Unconscionable terms: No objection to excluding truly unconscionable terms ; however, 
the proposed definition is too subjective to apply consistently without clearer guardrails. 
• Market Rate/FMV: Treat documented compliance with the Fees and Licensing Exceptions 
as sufficient to satisfy any market-rate requirement. 
• Contract of adhesion: Narrow the definition to align with common- law principles (both a 
meaningful power imbalance and a lack of realistic market alternatives) . We support and 
urge ASTP/ONC to adopt the following definition proposed by eHx:  
“Contract of adhesion” means: 
A contract that: (i) is presented on a standardized form without a realistic 
opportunity to negotiate material terms; (ii) is offered by a party with substantially 
superior bargaining power to a party that has no meaningful market alternative for 
obtaining the desired access, use, or exchange; and (iii) contains terms tha t are 
primarily for the benefit the drafting party. 
This definition appropriately limits the concept of a contract of adhesion to circumstances 
involving both a meaningful power imbalance and one -sided terms that favor the drafter. 
It ensures that ASTP/ONC targets agreements that restrict exchange through inequitable 
leverage, rather than standardized agreements that enable transparent , trusted 
interoperability. Cooperative, multi-party exchange agreements— such as those referenced 
above— do not reflect the types of  imbalances or extractive terms that HTI -5 seeks to 
address. 
II. Certification Program —  C-CDA Creation Performance (45 C.F.R. § 170.315(g)(6)) 
ASTP/ONC proposes to remove the C-CDA Creation Performance criterion. Although Contexture 
supports a FHIR -first future, the current ecosystem remains hybrid . For many P articipants, 
document-based exchange remains essential until such time as they can adopt FHIR. Accordingly, 
ASTP/ONC should not remove § 170.315(g)(6) at this time . Instead, we recommend a phased 
transition tied to measurable market readiness.  
Retention of the criterion does not divert resources from FHIR adoption; rather, it preserves 
baseline quality and operational stability during the transition to a FHIR-first ecosystem. 
Based on Contexture’s experience, the C-CDA Creation Performance criterion has been effective, 
and certification continues to play a meaningful role in maintaining baseline interoperability 
expectations. 

4 
 
Even small degradations in C-CDA can lead to processing delays, incomplete patient records, and 
reduced data usability for clinical, operational, and public health purposes. Minor deviations in 
vendor performance compound across networks, resulting in slower, less predictable document 
delivery and significantly increased normalization costs for community-based HIEs and HDUs.  
Contexture has almost  850 data contributors — particularly ambulatory clinics, skilled nursing 
facilities, and other LTPAC providers — that depend on C ‑CDA as their primary  and often only 
feasible method of structured exchange given resource constraints and limited FHIR capability in 
lower‑cost EHR products.  Removing the C ‑CDA Creation Performance criterion will not move 
these contributors to FHIR. Instead, providers who depend on C ‑CDA for care coordination will 
face greater time and financial burdens to obtain the necessary information, leaving them with less 
capacity to deliver care and ultimately diminishing the quality of care they provide to patients. 
C-CDA supports transitions of care, referrals, longitudinal  health record aggregation, encounter 
notifications, public health  activities, community -based coordination, and interoperability with 
legacy EHRs. Resource-constrained partners, including rural, tribal, and safety -net providers, as 
well as many state Medicaid programs that rely on CCDs for quality measurement, would be most 
affected. Contexture notes there may be increased opportunity to promote FHIR adoption 
amongst these partners through the Rural Health Transformation Program, authorized by the One 
Big Beautiful Bill Act. Contexture is committed to supporting increased FHIR utilization across our 
network where feasible and appropriate.  
Conclusion and Request for Agency Action 
Contexture supports HTI-5’s deregulatory goals and the transition to FHIR-based exchange. For 
the Manner Exception, Contexture recommends eliminating § 171.301(a)(2) and relying instead on 
compliance with the Fees and Licensing Exceptions as the operative guardrails.  For C -CDA 
Creation Performance, Contexture recommends a structured phase-out based on measurable 
readiness.

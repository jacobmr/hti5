Information Blocking - “Access” and “Use” Definitions 
In previous rulemaking, ASTP/ONC defined “access,” “exchange,” and “use” as closely 
related concepts. While these definitions have long encompassed automated methods, 
such as robotic process automation and autonomous artificial intelligence, stakeholder 
feedback and market experience have highlighted the need for clearer, explicit regulatory 
language. In response, ASTP/ONC now proposes to clarify that the definitions of access, 
exchange, and use apply equally to automated technologies, consistent with prior 
interpretations and longstanding alignment with HIPAA privacy and security requirements. 
 
Essentia Health appreciates the intent behind clarifying access and use definitions . 
However, we recommend careful consideration of proposed revisions to the 
definitions of “access” and “use,” particularly as they relate to automated 
technologies such as robotic process automation and autonomous artificial 
intelligence. Without clear delineation o f responsibilities, these changes could 
create confusion regarding liability and inadvertently expose providers to 
enforcement risk when systems lack the necessary capabilities. ASTP/ONC should 
consider tools or testing mechanisms that support program integrity while avoiding 
increased compliance burden on providers.

Removal or revision of certification criteria 
ASTP/ONC proposes to remove 34 and revise seven of the 60 existing certification 
criteria. According to ASTP/ONC, the proposed changes would reduce compliance costs 
for health IT developers and clinicians by eliminating obligations to design, test, certify , 
and maintain specific functionalities that have become operationally mature. T he 
proposed approach reflects ASTP/ONC’s determination that a substantial portion of the 
current program no longer meaningfully advances interoperability or incentivizes adoption 
of established functionality.  
 
Essentia Health understands the intent to focus the program on higher ‑value priorities. 
However, it is critical that any reductions remain aligned with CMS program requirements

Clinical Decision Support 
Over time, ASTP/ONC has updated its approach to clinical decision support to reflect 
advances in technology and the growing use of predictive tools and artificial intelligence  
(AI). Earlier certification requirements focused on ensuring evidence ‑based decision 
support with transparency into data sources, while more recent rulemaking transitioned 
the program to a broader decision support intervention framework intended to address 
emerging AI‑driven use cases. The legacy clinical decision support criterion wa s sunset 
as of January 1, 2025, and ASTP/ONC now proposes to formally remove that criterion 
and substantially narrow the clinical decision support intervention requirements.  
 
Specifically, the agency proposes to remove the clinical decision support certification 
criteria § 170.315 (a)(9) and to revise decision support interventions criteria § 170.315 
(b)(11). According to ASTP/ONC, these proposed changes would eliminate transparency 
and risk‑management provisions, reduce the scope of required data inputs, and remove 
references to certain legacy exchange and interaction checks, with the stated goal of 
reducing burden, lowering ongoi ng maintenance costs, and promoting innovation, 
particularly in the development of AI‑enabled tools. 
 
Essentia Health urges ASTP/ONC to proceed cautiously with proposed changes to 
clinical decision support criteria. Transparency around AI -enabled decision 
support tools remains essential to responsible procurement, clinician trust, and 
patient safety.  Retaining transparency requirements that inform clinicians about the

Transitions of Care 
Transitions of care requirements were originally established to support the standardized 
exchange of clinical information using Consolidated Clinical Document Architecture 
(C‑CDA) documents, including the ability to create, send, and receive summaries across 
care settings. Over time, these requirements have required ongoing maintenance and 
updates by developers, even as the underlying standards and exchange methods have 
matured. In this proposed rule, ASTP/ONC seeks to streamline the transitions of care 
criterion by removing certain C ‑CDA creation and transport requirements and narrowing 
the focus to validating receipt of clinical documents.  
 
Specifically, ASTP/ONC proposes to remove C -CDA generation and sending 
requirements from Transitions of Care, § 170.315 (b)(1), effective January 1, 2027.  It is 
important to note that these requirements are also tested in view, download, and transmit 
to third party, § 170.315 (e)(1) criteria. The agency should retain requirements in 
§ 170.315 (b)(1) and removing them from § 170.315 (e)(1).  
 
The agency’s intent of the proposed changes is  to reduce ongoing development and 
maintenance burden while preserving core functionality for receiving and supporting 
transitions of care.  Effective transitions of care a re essential to care coordination 
and successful value ‑based care. For example, ensuring patients have appropria te 
follow-up care  and communi ty support he lps to reduce hospital readmissions and 
maintain their health. As certification requirements are streamlined, it is important that 
high‑value functionality supporting real ‑world transitions of care workflows is preserved 
while reducing unnecessary administrative burdens.

Privacy and Security Certification Criteria 
ASTP/ONC proposes to eliminate the current privacy and security certification criteria and 
the related certification framework. These requirements were originally created to ensure 
certified health IT included basic features that could help support HIPAA c ompliance. 
Over time, the framework expanded to give developers flexibility in how those features 
were delivered, either within their products or through integration with other tools. 
ASTP/ONC has since concluded that these requirements have not meaningfully improved 
privacy or security and have instead added unnecessary burden and duplication for 
developers and health care providers. 
 
ASTP/ONC notes that certified health IT alone cannot ensure HIPAA compliance across 
complex, enterprise‑wide environments, and CMS programs have not required providers 
to use certified privacy and security features to meet their regulatory obligations. The  
agency also observe d that certification has often been misunderstood as a 
comprehensive security assurance, despite its limited scope. As a result, the agency has 
determined that the current approach has diverted resources away from more effective 
and fle xible security solutions. While proposing to remove these requirements, 
ASTP/ONC states it will continue to work to strengthen health care cybersecurity and may 
incorporate targeted security requirements directly into future certification criteria, 
particularly as the program shifts toward an API‑focused model. 
 
Despite these proposed changes, providers ultimately remain accountable for compliance 
with HIPAA privacy and security requirements and face increasing cybersecurity threats. 
Essentia Health urges ASTP/ONC to ensure that privacy and security capabilities 
remain a durable baseline expectation for certified technology. If privacy and security 
certification requirements are reduced, those changes should be paired with strengthened 
post-certification oversight and clear transparency regarding how certified products 
support essential security functions.  It is essential for CEHRT vendors to have strong 
privacy and security measures, and for the agency to ensure oversight by requiring these 
protocols to be disclosed as part of standard reporting and re-certification procedures.

Conclusion 
Essentia Health appreciates the opportunity to comment on ASTP/ONC’s Health Data, 
Technology, and Interoperability: ASTP/ONC Deregulatory Actions To Unleash 
Prosperity (HTI -5) proposed rule. We recognize the agency’s intent to modernize the 
Health IT Certi fication Program, reduce unnecessary burden, and better align 
requirements with a rapidly evolving health IT landscape. However, as these changes are 
considered, it is essential that certification updates remain closely aligned with CMS 
program requirements. Updates must preserve high‑value functionality that supports care 
coordination and patient safety and avoid shifting compliance or operational risk and costs 
to providers, especially those that serve rural communities.  
 
We encourage ASTP/ONC to continue engaging providers and value -based care 
organizations early and consistently as the program evolves and to proceed in a 
measured manner that supports interoperability, aligns with certification requirements, 
innovation, and the delivery of high-quality, value-based care.

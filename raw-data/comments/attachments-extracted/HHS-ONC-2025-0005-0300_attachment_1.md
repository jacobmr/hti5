overreliance

on

the

Infeasibility

Exception.

ASTP’s

proposal

would

the

effect

of

discouraging

regulated

actors

from

asserting

that

some

form

of

access

been

offered,

and

the

substantive

Revision of the “Manner Requested” Condition of the Manner Exception (§  
171.301(a))
Datavant supports the proposed revision of § 171.301(a) to clarify that the Manner Exception  
cannot

cover

contracts

that

are

not

at

market

rate,

are

contracts

of

adhesion,

or

include

unconscionable

terms.

This

proposed

revision

would

address

a

series

of

practices

in

which

some

regulated

actors

use

the

ﬁrst

prong

of

the

Manner

Exception

as

a

vehicle

for

competitive

restraint,

labor

market

restrictions,

and

intellectual

property

control.

Consider a hypothetical mandatory agreement presented as a precondition to access a  
health

IT

platform

on

behalf

of

mutual

provider

customers.

The

agreement

is

not

negotiable

and

is

presented

on

a

take-it-or-leave-it

basis.

Also,

the

developer

controls

both

the

terms

of

the

agreement

and

access

to

the

underlying

platform,

creating

a

classic

adhesion

dynamic.

Refusal

to

enter

into

the

agreement

or

seeking

to

negotiate

certain

terms

could

be

risky

or

operationally

catastrophic.

The

agreement’s

opening

section

declares

that

its

terms

are

“intended

to

satisfy

the

requirements

of

the

Manner

Exception

for

providing

access,

exchange,

and

use

of

EHI

in

the

‘manner

requested.’”

We

believe

this

is

the

precise

behavior

ASTP/ONC

targets

in

its

proposed

revision:

a

regulated

actor

using

a

mandatory

access

agreement

to

self-certify

Manner

Exception

coverage

for

terms

that

extend

far

beyond

interoperability

content.

In our experience, these types of agreements may often include provisions that bear no  
reasonable

relationship

to

interoperability

concepts

or

the

protection

of

legitimate

trade

secrets.

Instead,

these

agreements

may

contain

terms

that

function

as

instruments

of

7

competitive control. Such terms may include workforce non-compete restrictions,  
requirements

for

mandatory

physical

segregation

of

personnel,

intellectual

property

assignment

and

commercialization

restrictions,

broad

audit

rights,

hiring

restrictions,

and

more.

Taken individually, some of these provisions might be defended as important intellectual  
property

safeguards.

Taken

together,

they

form

an

interlocking

system

of

competitive

control.

The

proposed

revision

to

§

171.301(a)

would

directly

address

this

phenomenon

in

the

health

IT

industry

by

establishing

that

the

Manner

Exception

does

not

apply

to

contracts

of

adhesion

or

to

contracts

with

unconscionable

terms.

This

ensures

that

satisfying

the

“manner

requested”

condition

of

the

Manner

Exception

reﬂects

a

genuine

choice

by

the

requesting

party,

not

a

compelled

submission

to

conditions

imposed

by

the

actor

controlling

access

to

EHI.

We also generally support ASTP/ONC's proposal to require that agreements qualifying for  
the

Manner

Exception

reﬂect

market

rate

terms.

We

note,

however,

a

"market

rate"

standard

risks

codifying

the

rates

that

certain

market

incumbents

have

already

established

(which

may

reﬂect

rates

shaped

by

market

concentration

rather

than

by

the

cost

of

providing

access).

Current

pricing

models

for

non-standardized

APIs

illustrate

the

variability

of

market

rate

approaches.

For

example,

API

license

or

access

fees

often

include

substantial

ﬁxed

charges,

per-connection

fees,

and

recurring

per-requester

API

call

fees

that

function

as

data

tollgates.

Datavant encourages ASTP/ONC to supplement its proposed market rate standard with  
requirements

for

transparent,

cost-based

pricing

for

certain

APIs,

interfaces,

and

other

common

access

pathways

that

are

tied

to

documented,

veriﬁable

technical

expenses.

Fees

based

on

opportunity

cost,

data

control

leverage,

or

data

monetization

models

should

be

discouraged.

Standardized,

publicly

available

API

fee

schedules

applied

on

a

nondiscriminatory

basis

across

all

similarly

situated

requesters

would

be

transformative

for

the

health

IT

industry.

This

approach

would

complement

the

proposed

contract-of-adhesion

and

unconscionability

standards

by

addressing

the

ﬁnancial

dimension

of

the

same

problem:

some

regulated

actors

using

their

control

over

access

to

EHI

to

extract

value

that

bears

no

relationship

to

the

cost

of

providing

that

access.

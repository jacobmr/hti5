Indiana Health Information Exchange, Inc. (“IHIE”) appreciates the opportunity to provide 
comments on Health Data, Technology, and Interoperability: ASTP/ONC Deregulatory Actions to 
Unleash Prosperity Proposed Rule (HHS-ONC-2025-0005) (“Proposed Rule”).  IHIE is a non-profit 
organization and Health Data Utility (“HDU”) that administers and operates the Indiana Network 
for Patient Care (“INPC”) on behalf of, and provides Health Information Exchange (“HIE”) products 
and services to, its members and other eligible data recipients. 
 
IHIE combines, enhances, and exchanges electronic health information across care and service 
settings to support treatment, care coordination, quality improvement, and population and public 
health initiatives. Our statewide infrastructure integrates data from individual EHRs, providers, 
payors and the Indiana Department of Health, ensuring a more complete picture of patient care 
while equipping public health leaders with real-time insights to track outbreaks, identify trends, 
address disparities, and inform evidence-based policy.  In performing these activities, IHIE applies 
the data governance framework approved by the INPC members.  
 
While IHIE acknowledges the intent of the Office of the National Coordinator for Health IT (“ONC”) 
and the Assistant Secretary for Technology Policy (“ASTP”) for the revisions discussed in the 
Proposed Rule, we highlight, below, certain proposed changes to Information Blocking conditions 
and exceptions that would negatively impact IHIE’s ability to appropriately administer and 
operate the INPC and provide HDU and HIE services in a safe and equitable manner.   
 
1. Infeasibility Exception Revisions 
 
a. Proposal to Remove the “Third Party Seeking Modification Use” Condition 
 
ONC/ASTP is proposing to remove the “third party seeking modification use” condition from the 
Infeasibility Exception.  IHIE does not support its removal.  IHIE receives requests from non-

b. Proposed Changes to “Manner Exception Exhausted” Condition 
 
ONC/ASTP proposes changes to the condition to remove the “similarly situated” language or, in 
the alternative, removal of the condition in its entirety.  IHIE does not support change to or 
removal of this condition.  IHIE makes EHI available in different ways based on the nature of the 
requestor (for example, is the requestor a health care provider seeking EHI for treatment or a 
health plan seeking EHI for health care operations) due to the data governance rules that IHIE 
applies for compliance with law.  As such, IHIE must be able to treat similarly situated requestors 
in the same manner to ensure continued compliance rather than making EHI available in any 
manner that it has used with any individual or entity.

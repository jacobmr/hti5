Drawing on our experience at the intersection of healthcare data, consumer engagement, and 
technology innovation, Cedar respectfully submits the following comments to support and 
further inform key aspects of the proposed rule. 
I. Support for FHIR APIs as the Primary Interoperability Lever 
Cedar supports ASTP/ONCʼs emphasis on FHIR-based APIs as the primary mechanism for 
interoperability. Reliable, standardized APIs are critical not only for clinical data exchange, but 
also for downstream, patient-facing functions that directly affect affordability and access to 
care.

Modern healthcare billing and patient financial engagement processes increasingly extend 
beyond the traditional EHR interface. Real-time patient responsibility estimates, eligibility 
verification, financial assistance screening, and digital payment workflows require structured, 
programmatic access to clinical, coverage, and billing data. These functions cannot operate 
effectively through manual workflows or static file exchanges. 
To make these capabilities viable at scale, relevant FHIR data elements and API functionalities 
should be clearly defined within certification standards and implementation guidance. 
ASTP/ONC should therefore expand the USCDI standards to include standardized financial 
and billing data, including charge and invoice details, payment reconciliation transactions, 
cost-sharing and accumulator information, balance status indicators, payment plan terms, 
and patient/guarantor demographic and consent fields within the interoperability 
standards. Without these elements included, API-based interoperability will remain 
incomplete for the financial components of healthcare that increasingly affect patients 
directly. 
Finally, discretionary or inconsistent API implementation risks entrenching incumbent systems 
by making access to essential data contingent on proprietary arrangements rather than 
uniform, standards-based exchange. Greater clarity and consistency within the FHIR 
certification framework would help promote fair access, reduce integration costs, and better 
support patient-centered innovation. 
II. Clarifying That Automated and AIMediated API Access Qualifies as Permissible “Access 
and Useˮ 
Cedar also supports ASTP/ONCʼs efforts to clarify that automated and AI-mediated access to 
health information via APIs constitutes permissible “access and useˮ under the interoperability 
framework. This clarification is essential as healthcare systems increasingly rely on 
automation to deliver timely, personalized interactions for patients. 
To enable interoperable data exchange and maintain alignment across integrated health IT 
systems, we must continuously ingest and process billing, insurance,  eligibility, and payment 
data to remain accurate. These capabilities are not well served by manual, point-in-time data 
pulls. Clear confirmation that automated and AI-driven access is permitted will reduce 
uncertainty for providers and technology developers and help ensure that patients receive 
up-to-date, actionable information.

III. Addressing Contractual, Pricing, and Technical Practices That Functionally Restrict 
Interoperability 
Cedar appreciates ASTP/ONCʼs attention to contractual, pricing, and technical practices that 
may comply with interoperability requirements, yet materially restrict access in practice. 
Excessive API fees, opaque or inconsistently applied business rules, restrictive contractual 
provisions, and discretionary technical limitations can render access cost-prohibitive or 
operationally unworkable—even where providers seek to deploy interoperable, 
patient-centered solutions. 
These practices disproportionately burden application developers and patient-facing 
technologies that rely on scalable, standardized access across multiple systems. 
Interoperability policy should evaluate not only whether access is technically available, but 
whether it is provided on fair, reasonable, and operationally meaningful terms that support 
real-world implementation and patient-directed innovation. 
Conclusion 
Cedar strongly supports these efforts to advance API-driven interoperability, clarify 
automated access, and address practices that undermine effective data exchange. These 
reforms will improve patient experiences across the healthcare ecosystem. We appreciate the 
opportunity to provide input and welcome continued engagement with ASTP/ONC as this 
rulemaking proceeds.

  Deregulatory Actions to Unleash Prosperity 
 
Dear Mr. Lipinski: 
 
I have represented families in medical malpractice cases for 30 years. In that time, the evolution of medical 
records has been extraordinary. This did not occur as a result of deregulation; this happened only because of 
regulations which required more preservation, more interoperability, and more detail. HHS’s proposed rule would 
undermine the security of patient health information and eliminate requirements under the ONC Health IT 
Certiﬁcation Program. The goal of this program has always been to ensure the security, quality, and interoperability 
of electronic health records. Rather than promoting these gols, the proposed rule would result in the opposite. It 
would destroy transparency and accountability in our healthcare system at the expense of patient safety. 
 
Not only our client, but everyone deserves to feel safe in the healthcare space. Everyone deserves to know who is 
taking care of them, how those actions effect their health and their conditions, and that all of these deliberations 
and decisions are protected. In addition, the current system and regulations provides much needed patient safety 
guardrails. What ONC proposes would take these guardrails off. The people who would be hurt in this scenario are 
our families, the people we love, and the people who are at their most vulnerable seeking healthcare. 
 
The proposed rule revokes requirements to maintain the integrity of the electronic medical record and harms 
patient safety.  Anytime audit trails or logs are able to be edited, HER integrity and patient safety suffers. The 
proposed rule would expose EHRs to manipulation, and fail to ensure their accuracy and the integrity of the 
information recorded in them. Nothing about this is safe for patients. 
 
Moreover, the proposed rule would affect the ability fo ensure the safe use of AI and prevent software errors which 
can lead to medical decision-making errors. A culture of shadow medical records has arisen in many health 
systems and the proposed Rule would make it acceptable to hide medical records on which providers rely to make 
decisions about patient care. This is not how anyone wants their healthcare to be. It must be reliable and the 
record of what was done must also be reliable. The proposed rule would eviscerate patient safety. 
 
For all of these reasons, I urge HHS to reject the proposed modiﬁcation to the ONC Certiﬁcation Program. 
 


 
Respctfully submitted, 
 
Theresa M. Blanco, Esquire

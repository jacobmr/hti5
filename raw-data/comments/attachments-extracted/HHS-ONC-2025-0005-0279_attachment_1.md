On behalf of Real Time Medical Systems (“Real Time”), thank you for the opportunity to

The information blocking prohibition that Congress established is critical to the appropriate 
utilization of timely patient data to create meaningful improvements in patient health. 
Innovations in data analysis, health surveillance, and other advances allow for better patient 
outcomes. Restriction of access to patient data, motivated by actors’ financial or anti-competitive 
interests, penalizes American patients and the American healthcare system overall.  
 
Real Time is eager to support HHS’ efforts to better define and enforce this statutory prohibition, 
so that Congress can deliver on their promise to ensure meaningful protections in the access, use 
and exchange of health data.  
 
In response to HTI-5, Real Time offers several comments: 
• Amendments to the manner exception, and references to “market rates” 
• Scope of data impacted by information blocking 
• Private right of action

Amendments to the manner exception and “market rates” 
In HTI-5, HHS proposes to add additional protections related to the manner exceptions, 
specifically related to demands actors can assert upon requestors under the “manner requested” 
condition. 
 
Existing regulations permit actors under specific circumstances to charge fees of requestors for 
the access to information stored by electronic health record platforms. HHS proposes to require 
that fees be set at the “market rate.” In addition, HHS mandates that the contract met between the 
actor and requestor cannot be “a contract of adhesion” and “must not contain unconscionable 
terms.” 
 
Real Time appreciates HHS’s efforts to expand the regulation to ensure that an actor cannot 
unfairly leverage its position as the storer of patient data to exact undue fees and terms upon 
entities requesting access to that data.  
 
Real Time is concerned that the “market rate” could still permit actors with sizable market 
influence to exact fees that rise beyond the reasonable amount. Unfortunately, for many EHR 
applications, one actor controls a majority of the market share.  To the degree the “market rate” is 
set by a negotiation between an actor and a requestor, negotiated fees may reflect the relative 
market position of a small innovator against a potentially monopolistic, entrenched entity.  
 
We appreciate that HHS has included additional language related to “unconscionable terms” and 
“contract of adhesion.” Real Time is eager to understand how HHS intends to implement that 
language in a manner that ensures an actor cannot engage in de facto information blocking by 
charging unwieldy fees or fees that do not comport with the Fees Exception at 45 C.F.R. § 
171.302.  
 
As we are certain you are aware, actors are already well paid for capturing, storing and 
maintaining EHR data, as well as interoperability, as it is a core EHR function, and therefore we 
question whether additional fees to actors are required for appropriately sharing data. Any 
additional costs should be incorporated into the software licensing and use fees. 
 
Alternatively, we encourage HHS to consider adopting more specific standards for allowable 
fees. Rather than deferring to market rates in a sector routinely pitting small innovators against 
sizable entities, HHS could level the playing field by tying fees to actual, incremental costs 
incurred by actors to transfer data, with an allowance for a modest profit margin.  Otherwise, 
higher, “market-based” fees would unfairly stifle competition and eliminate creative innovation.

Scope of Impacted Data 
HHS has implemented regulations that outline exceptions of the broad-based information 
blocking prohibition. 
 
In addition to outlining the instances in which exceptions are permissible, Real Time encourages 
HHS to ensure there is clarity about the breadth of data subject to the rules.  
 
HHS should make clear that information blocking exceptions do not permit actors to limit access 
to aspects of data held by actors. Electronic Health Information (EHI) should be treated 
holistically as a single field of information that should be open to use and exchange.  
 
Private Right of Action 
Real Time is greatly encouraged by public commitments by which the Department of Health and 
Human Services (HHS) has signaled more significant enforcement efforts for information 
blocking. As a fervent supporter of the information blocking prohibition that Congress 
established, Real Time is grateful to see the agency move more aggressively to support the 
bipartisan intent of the chamber.  
 
However, Real Time would also encourage HHS to consider whether there are avenues for the 
agency to facilitate a private right of action against actors engaged in information blocking. 
Understanding that HHS has finite resources for investigations into individual cases, the 
opportunity for requestors to seek relief through the courts could supplement rather than 
complicate HHS’ ability to ensure an ecosystem for health data that prioritizes the health of 
patients.  
 
We appreciate the work that HHS has undertaken to enhance the impact of the information 
blocking prohibition Congress set forth in the 21st Century Cures Act. Please do not hesitate to 
contact us if we can answer any questions.

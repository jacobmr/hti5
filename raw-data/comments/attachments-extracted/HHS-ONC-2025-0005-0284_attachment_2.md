Comment on Patient Portal Capabilities to Support Parent and Legal Guardian 
Access to Minor Records 
Summary of Comment 
We appreciate ASTP/ONC’s stated goals in HTI‑5 to reduce unnecessary burden while 
strengthening patient access to electronic health information and preventing misuse of 
information blocking exceptions. We write to recommend that ASTP/ONC include clear 
expectations for health information technology developers regarding standard, configurable 
support for parent and legal guardian proxy access to minor patient records within certified 
patient portal and administrative applications. 
Without explicit guidance, health IT developers can continue to rely on broad, record‑wide proxy 
models that over‑restrict access, undermine legislative and regulatory intent, and shift technical 
and operational burden onto provider organizations. These gaps are increasingly exposed as 
states enact laws expanding parental access while preserving minors’ legal rights. 
Problem Statement 
In practice, many certified patient portals offer only coarse proxy access models for minors that 
restrict large portions of the medical record across all encounters once a minor reaches a certain 
age. While these models may protect confidentiality, they do so by blocking significantly more 
information than state or federal law requires. 
Health IT developers often assert that more granular controls are “possible” through 
customization. However, customization requires provider organizations to alter or extend 
software in ways that are not part of the developer’s supported foundational model, frequently 
disabling automation, increasing manual clinical workflows, and creating significant risk during 
routine version upgrades. This approach is not scalable, not safe, and not aligned with 
ASTP/ONC’s deregulatory objectives. 
Recommendation 
ASTP/ONC should clarify in HTI‑5 that certified health IT products are expected to provide certain 
baseline patient portal capabilities through vendor‑supported configuration rather than 
customization. These capabilities should be delivered as standard code within the developer’s

supported product model so they can be enabled by provider organizations without creating 
upgrade or maintenance risk. 
We recommend ASTP/ONC articulate expectations that patient portals and associated 
administrative tools support the following capabilities through vendor‑supported configuration: 
1. Encounter‑level or service‑level proxy segmentation 
Health IT products should support the ability to limit a parent or legal guardian’s access at the 
encounter or service level when required by law, rather than restricting access by broadly 
withholding categories of information across the minor patient’s entire record. 
2. Downstream consistency of access controls 
When access to an encounter or service is restricted, the same restriction should 
automatically and consistently apply to downstream elements associated with that encounter, 
including test results, orders, referrals, visit documentation, and follow‑up instructions 
presented in the patient portal. 
3. Preservation of the minor patient’s electronic access 
Health IT products should preserve a minor patient’s electronic access to the minor’s own 
medical record, except where a narrow and lawful exception applies, even when parent or 
guardian proxy access is restricted. 
4. Multi‑jurisdiction configurability 
Health IT products should support the ability to apply differing state and federal requirements 
related to minor consent, confidentiality, and parental access through configuration alone, 
including for provider organizations operating across multiple states. 
Clarification on Configuration Versus Customization 
ASTP/ONC should distinguish between vendor‑supported configuration and customization. 
Configuration refers to functionality that is delivered, documented, supported, and maintained by 
the health IT developer as part of its generally available product offerings and can be enabled 
through administrative settings. Customization refers to provider‑built code, workflows, or logic 
that fall outside the developer’s supported foundational model and must be re‑engineered or 
revalidated during upgrades. 
Provider organizations should not be required to implement customization to meet access 
expectations related to minor proxy access. Shifting this responsibility to providers undermines 
system stability, increases operational risk, and conflicts with the objective of reducing burden. 
Alignment With HTI‑5 Goals 
Including these expectations aligns with HTI‑5’s objectives by: 
• Promoting patient and family access to electronic health information without over‑restriction 
• Preventing misuse of technical limitations as a de facto information blocking mechanism

• Reducing provider burden by placing responsibility for foundational capabilities on health IT

• Supporting scalable, standards‑based solutions that work across jurisdictions without bespoke

Conclusion 
We encourage ASTP/ONC to use HTI‑5 to clarify that supporting lawful parent and legal guardian 
access to minor records is a core patient access function that must be addressed through 
standard, configurable health IT capabilities. Clear expectations at the federal level will drive 
consistent, safe, and sustainable solutions that benefit patients, families, providers, and 
developers alike.

The Digital Quality Implementers Community (DQIC) appreciates this opportunity to review and provide
feedback on Health Data, Technology, and Interoperability: ASTP/ONC Deregulatory Actions to Unleash
Prosperity (RIN: 0955-AA09, Docket No. 2025-23896).
The Digital Quality Implementers Community (DQIC) is a collaborative coalition dedicated to advancing digital
quality measurement through open standards like HL7® FHIR® and Clinical Quality Language. DQIC members
are active in the HL7® Clinical Quality Information workgroup, have participated in Connectathons throughout
the lifecycle of electronic Clinical Quality Measures, and currently support quality reporting implementations
using both QDM and emerging FHIR-based approaches.
We commend ASTP/ONC for proposing significant deregulatory actions that reduce burden while advancing
interoperability, including removal of outdated certification criteria and the clear articulation of an "America's
FHIR®-Forward Future" policy. We particularly support the proposed removal of C-CDA creation requirements
from § 170.315(b)(1) and § 170.315(g)(6) to enable industry to redirect efforts toward FHIR®-based solutions.
However, this FHIR-forward approach contains a critical inconsistency in quality reporting that must be
addressed. DQIC strongly urges ASTP/ONC to consider revising § 170.315(c)(3) to permit technology-neutral
quality reporting through either QRDA or FHIR®-based implementation guides, mirroring the regulatory
structure already established for electronic case reporting.  
Additionally, in the information blocking provisions, DQIC strongly supports the proposed removal of the
TEFCA Manner Exception and the Third-Party Seeking Modification Use Condition and urges complete
removal rather than revision of the Manner Exception Exhausted Condition to eliminate provisions that have
been systematically exploited to obstruct legitimate data access for quality measurement and innovation. Our
detailed recommendations follow.

I. Certification Provisions and Functional Requirements
Recommendation: Apply Technology-Neutral Standards to Quality Reporting (§ 170.315(c)(3)),
Mirroring Transitions of Care Modernization (§ 170.315(b)(1))  
We strongly support retaining § 170.315(c)(3) for clinical quality measures reporting. However, ASTP/ONC's
approach contains a fundamental inconsistency that undermines the stated "FHIR-Forward Future" policy. We
strongly urge ASTP/ONC to revise §§ 170.315(c)(1), (c)(2), and (c)(3) to permit FHIR®-based quality reporting
as a voluntary alternative to QRDA.  
Currently, mandatory QRDA requirements persist at every stage of the CQM pipeline: export (c)(1), import
(c)(2), and transmission (c)(3). ASTP/ONC has already established a model in § 170.315(f)(5) for electronic
case reporting, where either legacy or FHIR®-based alternatives satisfy the requirement. In the proposed rule,
ASTP/ONC states legacy document formats "detract from industry efforts to move toward FHIR-based
solutions" This statement applies with equal force to QRDA, which shares identical limitations: no data
element-level retrieval, no real-time exchange, and incompatibility with contemporary web-based
architecture.  
. We urge the same technology-neutral approach here. The HL7® FHIR® DEQM Implementation Guide provides
functionally equivalent capabilities across all three criteria. NCQA is already transitioning HEDIS® measures to
FHIR-based formats, and the industry-supported Advancing Digital Quality Reporting Using Regulated
Endpoints whitepaper demonstrates that nearly half of universal foundation and screening measures for
provider-reported programs can be reported today using only § 170.315(g)(10) endpoints. QRDA would
remain fully acceptable; this change creates a pathway, not a mandate— and closes the most significant gap
between HTI-5's stated FHIR®-forward direction and its actual regulatory effect.  
 Without this change, HTI-5 modernizes clinical exchange while trapping quality reporting in the identical
legacy architecture ASTP/ONC explicitly seeks to phase out.

II. Information Blocking Provisions

1. Strong Support for Removal of TEFCA Manner Exception (§ 171.403)
   DQIC strongly supports the proposed removal of the TEFCA Manner Exception (§ 171.403). While we
   recognize TEFCA's role in advancing national interoperability, this exception has created a regulatory pathway
   that actors can misuse to restrict data access and stifle innovation. By eliminating this provision, ASTP/ONC
   will prevent TEFCA from functioning as a ceiling on interoperability or as a mechanism for creating exclusive
   data networks that stifle third-party innovators and quality measurement entities.
2. Strong Support for Removal of the Third-Party Seeking Modification Use Condition
   (§ 171.204(a)(3))
   DQIC strongly supports the removal of the third-party seeking modification use condition from the
   Infeasibility Exception. As noted in the wording for the proposed rule, this provision has been used to deny
   legitimate access requests from quality measurement organizations, health information exchanges, and other
   third parties with valid use cases. The condition's vague "modification" standard has allowed actors to claim
   information blocking protection when refusing to provide data in formats necessary for quality reporting,
   population health analysis, and care coordination. Removing this condition will eliminate a significant barrier
   that has impeded the flow of information critical to measuring and improving healthcare quality. This will not
   add burden and will enable information to be used across the health care ecosystem

3. Recommendation to Remove, Not Revise, the “Infeasibility Exception – Manner Exception
   Exhausted Condition” (§ 171.204(a)(4))
   We appreciate ASTP/ONC's proposed revisions to narrow the Manner Exception Exhausted Condition;
   however, DQIC urges complete removal of § 171.204(a)(4) rather than modification. We believe even with
   revised language replacing "same" with "analogous" and "substantial number" with "any," this condition
   remains fundamentally susceptible to manipulation. For example, actors may be able to strategically fragment
   their service offerings to ensure no two implementations are "analogous," enabling them to claim this
   exception to deny access to competitors and quality measurement entities. We strongly recommend
   adopting the alternative proposal to remove § 171.204(a)(4) entirely and rely on other, more targeted
   exceptions to address genuine infeasibility concerns. This removal is critical to preventing continued
   obstruction of legitimate data access requests essential for quality measurement and innovation.
   Conclusion
   DQIC appreciates ONC’s commitment to reducing regulatory burden, promoting interoperability, and
   supporting innovation in health IT. We believe the proposed deregulatory actions, if finalized with the
   recommendations above, will advance the nation’s digital health infrastructure and improve the quality and
   accessibility of care.

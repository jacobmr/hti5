As the largest digital cloud fax platform in the United States, Consensus Cloud Solutions has supported 
the exchange of health information for more than 25 years. Throughout this time, we have expanded to 
multiple interoperability protocols and observed firsthand both the challenges and opportunities in 
enabling secure, trusted, and efficient data exchange. Fax remains widely used across all care settings, 
particularly by providers in rural and underserved communities and by organizations operating disparate 
systems. 
 
Despite its abundance, the content exchanged through fax, PDFs, and scanned images remains 
overwhelmingly unstructured. Providers must manually review and extract essential patient 
information, which is an inefficient process that contributes to delays, errors, and unnecessary 
administrative burden. Today, an estimated 70% of all healthcare data remains unstructured, limiting 
clinicians' ability to access the complete information required to inform high-quality care.
 
 
We commend ASTP/ONC for moving the nation closer to a system in which electronic health 
information (EHI) can be accessed in a structured, standardized format, and for strengthening 
information blocking regulations in HTI-5. While the rule is deregulatory in many respects, it rightly 
reinforces strong expectations that EHI must remain accessible to approved entities, including through 
automated and AI-enabled means.

Information Blocking Infeasibility and Manner Exception 
 
Consensus believes the "Infeasibility” or “Manner” Exception in the HTI-5 proposed rule reinforces that 
an actor may only claim infeasibility if it does not already provide analogous access, exchange, or use of 
the requested EHI to any other individual or entity. This clarification is essential to support 
reinforcement of information blocking regulations.   
 
Technological advancements, such as intelligent data extraction, are reducing the challenges associated 
with identifying clinically relevant data buried within unstructured documents. When critical patient 
information does not populate the longitudinal record, the patient's treatment history remains 
incomplete.
 
 
We applaud ASTP/ONC for recognizing the role of intelligent extraction technologies in making patient 
data more discoverable. Consensus Cloud Solutions partners with multiple EHR developers, embedding 
this capability directly into EHR workflows. Once an EHR uses such functionality internally, it establishes 
a clear technical feasibility baseline and should not claim that providing the same data externally is 
"infeasible." 
 
Clarification of "Autonomous AI" in Access and Use 
 
HTI-5 proposes revising the definitions of "access" and "use" to expressly include automated and 
autonomous AI systems. This clarification prevents vendors from asserting that AI-generated or AI-
extracted data falls outside the scope of interoperability or information blocking requirements.
 
 
We support this change, as our extraction technologies help ensure that data accessible through AI 
processes is also accessible to patients and other authorized requesters. If an AI system used by an EHR 
can access patient data, then that data is, by definition, unlocked and must be shareable.
 
 
Removal of the "Manner Exception Exhausted" Loophole 
 
We support ASTP/ONC's proposal to remove the "Manner Exception Exhausted" condition, which has 
allowed actors to provide lower-quality, less usable data (such as static faxes) despite having the 
capability to offer higher-quality formats.

Transitions of Care 
 
The HTI-5 proposed rule initiates a shift toward a FHIR-first framework for transitions of care while 
streamlining or revising existing certification criteria. While FHIR offers greater granularity and flexibility, 
the Consolidated-Clinical Document Architecture (C-CDA) remains widely used and necessary for many 
care settings, especially in long-term and post-acute care. 
 
As patients transition from one care setting to the other, missing continuity of care information can lead 
to delayed care and administrative burden leading to bad health outcomes.  
  
Consensus technologies can support both FHIR and C-CDA, enabling providers to receive unstructured 
content in a structured format that can speed treatment without the disruption of data entry. As HTI-5 
states, and Consensus supports, continued use for C-CDA is essential today as many providers cannot 
ingest FHIR messages. 
 
 
Removal of the "Third-Party Modification" Barrier 
 
Consensus Cloud Solutions also supports removal of the third-party modification condition from the 
Infeasibility Exception. This change will prevent EHR developers from blocking third-party technologies 
from writing data back into the EHR. Closing these gaps will improve care continuity, reduce delays, and 
enhance patient follow-up.
 
 
Conclusion 
 
Consensus Cloud Solutions appreciates ASTP/ONC's commitment to promoting meaningful 
interoperability through the HTI-5 proposed rule. Our technologies can help operationalize the intent of 
the rule and reduce the administrative burden for providers associated with unstructured data and 
allow for all patients to have access to their data.
